## Part 2 — sermorelin and gonadorelin

- **Sermorelin:** "Once an approved drug (Geref, 1997–2008); compounded legally today." These are your
  words and your years, and the record supports them. GEREF, NDA 20-443 (the GH-deficiency vials), was
  "initially approved on September 26, 1997". The sponsor told FDA in letters of July and December 2008
  that the products "[were] being discontinued". FDA withdrew approval effective June 18, 2009 at its
  request (78 FR 14095; 74 FR 23407; openFDA). The FDA Status row keeps 12d's withdrawal detail.
- **Gonadorelin**, the sibling with 12d's "discontinued FDA-approved drug" line: "Once an approved drug
  (Factrel, 1982–2004; Lutrepulse, 1989–2002); compounded legally today." FACTREL (NDA 018123) was
  approved September 30, 1982, and its last strength was discontinued in June 2004. LUTREPULSE KIT
  (NDA 019687) was approved October 10, 1989 and discontinued in June–July 2002. Sources: Drugs@FDA and
  the Orange Book cumulative supplements of December 2002 and December 2004.
- Both stamps are COMPOUNDING PHARMACY in white ink. The 13 pair pages for the two carry the new lines.

## Part 3 — truth remainders

**a) Vendor names.**

- **Where they were.** Twenty pages carried them. The eight 12d flagged: Klotho, FOXO4-DRI, PDA,
  waglerin-1, zuclomiphene, MariTide, bimagrumab, ecnoglutide. Twelve more: cardarine, DSIP,
  enclomiphene, FGL, FLGR-242, GW-0742, IGF-1 LR3, LL-37, Melanotan II, MOTS-c, testosterone,
  tripeptide-29. Also the BPC-157 + TB-500 pair page and `/stacks/data.json`.
- **What was named.** Reagent and reference-standard suppliers, research-chemical shops, clinics, med
  spas and telehealth sellers, and the bulk-powder companies in FDA's drug directory. No name remains;
  the site-wide scan now finds none.
- **How the text changed.** The sellers are described ("a laboratory reagent supplier", "a clinic",
  "a telehealth seller"). Where a quote contained a seller's name, the name is in [brackets].
- **References.** 16 seller references are gone, two of them replaced by a non-seller source.
  Ecnoglutide's sequence and formula now come from PubChem (CID 162625103), as does FGL's molecular
  weight (CID 16200289). FDA's NDC listings on PDA are cited by number.

**b) The "February 2026 reclassification".**

- **The record.** I checked it against FDA's own 503A categories file, in its archived April 15 and
  April 22 versions and the current May 14 version, and against NPR's report of the remarks:
  - **April 15, 2026:** FDA gave notice that 12 peptides would leave Category 2 "because the
    nominations were withdrawn by the nominators": BPC-157, LL-37, Dihexa acetate, Emideltide (DSIP),
    Epitalon, injectable GHK-Cu, KPV, PEG-MGF, Melanotan II, MOTS-c, Semax and TB-500.
  - **April 22:** they were removed. That left them in no category, not Category 1.
  - **May 14:** non-injectable GHK-Cu went back into Category 1.
- **The rewrites.** 161 sentences on 103 pages now state, for each compound, whether it was among the
  12 and where FDA's May 14 list puts it, cited. The pages that described the buzz itself now report
  the remarks as remarks: "public remarks, reported, not a rule". Nine neighboring sentences that
  leaned on "that announcement" were rewritten too.
- **References.** The four fabricated ones ("Public Statement on Peptide Reclassification, February 27,
  2026" and an HHS "announcement") are replaced by FDA's list or by NPR's report, here and in
  `/stacks/data.json`. FDA's list is now a reference on 104 pages and NPR's report on 14. That includes
  12 pages that already cited "FDA notice April 15, 2026" without listing it.
- **One distinction kept.** MGF is in 503A Category 3; it was its pegylated form, PEG-MGF, that FDA
  removed. MK-677 and kisspeptin-10 stay in Category 2, and their pages say so.

**c) GH Stack.** The hero line and the Gist box now read: never tested as a pair; similar pairs gave
about twice the GH of the stronger peptide alone. Specifically:

- **Hexarelin with GHRH:** a GH AUC of 4,259 against 2,201 for hexarelin and 792 for GHRH, "higher
  than the arithmetic sum" (Arvat et al., 1997, PMID 9005975; six young volunteers).
- **GHRP-2 with GHRH:** "not significantly different from the sum" (Tiulpakov et al., 1995, PMID
  7586605; eight men).

Both are cited and pass the citation gate. Humanin's 1000-fold figure and bromantane's half-life are
untouched, as the brief says.

**d) The claim audit.** `gsc-export/claim-audit.csv` holds 2,282 sentences, and **every one has a
verdict: 0 skipped, 0 unjudged.** The breakdown: 2,002 supported, 152 not checkable (each with its
reason: 39 because the cited paper has no abstract, 113 judged by hand), 59 overstated, 69
contradicted. `data/claim-judgments.json` holds 2,282 judgments.

## DECISIONS

1. **Spec §7 has no stamp treatment** (it covers the reading theme, the mark and the vial label), so
   the colors come from the brief: green `--approved`, white ink `--ink`, red `--red`. A new tone,
   `ink`, carries COMPOUNDING PHARMACY.
2. **Evidence verdicts are not legal stamps.** A compound hero's own stamp is its evidence verdict
   (spec rule 3), so Preclinical, Phase III and the like stay. Only the three verdicts that were
   legal statuses changed. Their heroes now carry the legal stamp itself, generated from the data like
   a stack's, with the stance line right under it. Their evidence levels are pinned in
   `EVIDENCE_OVERRIDE`, so the meter didn't move.
3. **The 40 mid-level verdicts went back to gold.** 12d's green for COMPOUNDING PHARMACY had spread to
   them. Gold is the spec's color for them, and it keeps green for FDA APPROVED alone.
4. **"Checked" dates.** The stamp restyle is a generator change, so it moved no page's date, except
   the five pages whose own text changed in Part 1: the three heroes, About and the guide. This follows
   the rule that template and data changes never move the date. The corrections in Parts 3a–c moved
   the dates of the pages they corrected, as 12d's did. 133 pages show 3 Oct 2026.
5. **The changelog.** The site-wide wording change is one "site" entry. Every page-level change is a
   correction on `/corrections.html`: **142 corrections today**, and 513 entries in all.
6. **Sermorelin's years stand.** The record supports 1997 and 2008, and the two nuances are logged in
   `card-data.csv` rather than changed: Geref Diagnostic dates from 1990, and an Orange Book supplement
   listed the vials as discontinued from 2003, while the 2013 determination dates the discontinuation
   to 2008. Gonadorelin had two brands, so its line names both, in your form.
7. **What counts as a vendor.** A business that sells the compound: reagent and reference-standard
   suppliers, research-chemical shops, clinics, med spas, telehealth sellers, bulk-powder labelers.
   Three things are not vendors: drug developers named for their trials (Amgen, Lilly and the like),
   testing labs, and people. Seller-sourced facts about a seller's own product stay, attributed to "a
   laboratory supplier" and the like. A scientific fact that rested on a seller's document now rests
   on PubChem.
8. **The February rewrite states only what is verified for each compound.** That is membership in
   April's 12 and the compound's category on the May 14 list. Sentences about the buzz call it "public
   remarks, reported, not a rule". The podcast's release date (February 27) is in the research notes
   but I did not confirm it at the source, so the pages say "late February", which is NPR's wording.
9. **Reference-only fix beyond the brief.** Twelve pages already cited "FDA notice April 15, 2026"
   without listing it. They now list it, with a correction each.
10. **The COA edge case.** An all-empty slip keeps its six lines, because an empty slip says nothing.
    The prompt and schema are unchanged, so no preview fixture run was needed; the gate was the
    re-score.
11. **The SVG favicon uses the small K too.** Browsers that support it draw it at 16 px, so it had to
    read at 16 px.
12. **No icon links on four files, by design.** The embed (a framed widget with no site head) and the
    two meta-refresh redirect stubs carry none.
13. **Vercel CLI not upgraded mid-session** (60.1.3 installed, 62.2.0 published). The deploy runs
    through the documented git-less copy, and upgrading during a deploy session carries risk for no
    gain today.
