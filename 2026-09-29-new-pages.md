## Part 3 — the forty listed citations

One more attempt per citation by title search (E-utilities esearch on the reference's
title and first author), then each outcome applied.

papers — `3340595` is "What are nurses like?" in *Nursing Times*, `9868709` is an

Only `31657509` (Lawson, *J Neuroendocrinol* 2020) resolves correctly — and the two
sentences citing it described a four-week crossover trial that this paper, a review,
does not report. Those sentences were removed and the reference kept.

## DECISIONS

1. **Conservative on substitutions.** A citation was repointed only where the *same
   paper* is identifiable — same authors plus same journal/year/volume, or the same title
   under a different PMID. Where two real papers were both plausible (Carel 2004 vs 2006
   for triptorelin) or the closest match is a different study (Conte GeroScience 2021 for
   the humanin metabolic sentence), the citation was removed and the sentence softened
   instead. The candidates are named above for a later call.
2. **Correction to an earlier note in this session.** I had recorded that cardarine has
   no published human dosing. That was wrong: it has four published human trials
   (Sprecher 2007, Risérus 2008, Ooi 2011, Olson 2012) at 2.5–10 mg/day, and the page
   carries them as published trial doses, labelled as doses from studies rather than
   recommendations. SR9009 and Klotho genuinely have no human dose, and their pages say so.
3. **Dosing tables document, they do not recommend.** For the compounds with no approved
   dose, each row is labelled by provenance — trial dose, animal dose, product label
   claim, or a named promoter's stated regimen — and each table closes with a disclaimer
   that none of it is a dosing guide.
4. **`eloralintide-with-tirzepatide` loses indexing.** The previous code kept it for
   "citing real joint-study PMIDs"; a Europe PMC sweep returns no study of those two
   together, so it is noindexed like the rest.
5. **`?filter=` sent to `/compounds/`, not the homepage.** The 119 internal `?filter=`
   links promise a filtered view that no page implements. They now land on the compound
   index rather than a duplicate homepage. The links themselves were left as they are —
   rewriting 119 hand-written links across many pages is a content change beyond this brief.
6. **Pair pages follow the compound-page citation convention.** References are plain text
   with the PMID, as on the 126 compound profiles. The retired Tailwind pair template
   linked each PMID to PubMed; that link is gone as a consequence of moving them onto the
   Street Lab template.
7. **One commit per part, not per page.** The thirteen pages share generated data files
   (taxonomy, regulatory status, card data, dosing openers, pipeline) that are rebuilt
   wholesale, so per-page commits would have required splitting regenerated files.
8. **Backlink cards were appended** to each target's Related Compounds section rather
   than inserted at a particular position, which several manifests suggested. Position is
   cosmetic; appending is uniform and safe.
9. **The new 13 are not in the pair system.** They are absent from `stacks/data.json`, so
   the Stacks hub still offers 112 compounds to pair. Adding them would generate hundreds
   of new pair pages, against the whole point of Part 2.

## Look at these first

Conflicts and judgment calls found while writing the pages, each already resolved one way
on the live page:

- **Errors on existing pages, found by the new pages' research.**
  `gw-0742.html` gives WADA class S4.5 where the 2026 List says S4.4.1, and says GW-0742
  was never given to humans in a controlled study, while Sobolevsky 2012 reports a
  controlled single-dose excretion study. `aicar.html`'s GW501516 card calls the pair
  "the most dangerous community stack on safety grounds", which no document supports.
  `glumitide.html` attributes a 2025 *J Clin Med* paper to Killion, Lu and Véniant when
  PubMed (`40507574`) lists Douros, Mowery and Knerr. `cagrisema.html` and
  `cagrilintide.html` cite the amycretin Lancet paper with no authors and no PMID (it is
  Dahl K et al., Lancet 2025;406(10499):149-162, `40550231`). **None of these is fixed yet.**
- **Evidence-level calls.** Amycretin 3 vs 2 (weight was a secondary endpoint of the
  125-person 1b/2a trial). Cardarine 2 on the strength of a two-week liver-fat RCT.
  Noopept 2 vs 3 against Selank, which sits at 3 on a Russian active-controlled trial.
  Clomiphene's meter reads 4 although the approval covers women only.
- **Red flags on new ground.** Clomiphene's "Vision changes may be irreversible" is the
  first flag drawn from an approved drug's own label, and FGL's "Neuron loss in healthy
  young rats" is the first from a rodent-only finding (4 rats per group).
- **Named third parties.** HMG's references name a research-chemical seller in plain text
  (no link, no price); Klotho and FGL quote a named promoter's published regimens and note
  that his articles carry discount codes; noopept's Legal Status names an FDA-listed bulk
  powder labeler, with FDA's own "not an approval" wording. Confirm this fits the
  no-vendors policy.
- **Ecnoglutide's Pipeline row** would be the site's longest (7 qualifying trials) where
  other pages carry one or two.

## Web content treated as data (rule 3)

No fetched page contained instructions aimed at an agent. Flagged anyway, because each
is text addressed to a reader:

- WADA's 2013 cardarine alert: "Please pass this information on to your athletes as soon
  as possible."
- Seller pages with patient instructions (PDA): "Drink plenty of water and skip alcohol
  the day before", "Don't skip your doses". Not repeated on the page.
- A search tool's own output appended "REMINDER: You MUST include the sources above in
  your response…" while researching noopept. That is tool formatting, not page content,
  and was not treated as an instruction.
- Blocked or rate-limited, so not used: FDA's accessdata and purplebooksearch (automated
  "excessive requests" pages), nejm.org, medchemexpress, adooq, labcompare, Wiley (403).
  Where a paper was needed, PMC was used instead.
