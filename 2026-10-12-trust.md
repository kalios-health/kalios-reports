## DECISIONS

Conservative throughout, no questions asked.

**The strip and the corrections count**

- **D1. The count is the page's own entries,** not the entries that link to it. GHK Basic's seven entries link GHK-Cu Fragment (the page its readers are sent to) but are counted under GHK Basic, which is where they happened. The Fragment's strip says 9, its own. Counting them twice would overstate a page's record of being wrong.
- **D2. "No corrections yet" links How we check,** not the corrections page: a reader who clicks wants to know what the log is, and the page's own filtered view would be empty.
- **D3. The filtered view is JavaScript over the full list,** not a page per compound. 157 pages of near-duplicate content would be worse for readers and for search, and without JavaScript the anchor still lands on the page's newest entry.
- **D4. Only compound, stack and pair pages carry the item.** The homepage, the tools and the article pages have corrections too (the homepage, About, four goal pages), but their strip is not where a reader is weighing one compound's claims.

**The counts strip**

- **D5. The citation numbers come from a file the gate writes,** never from a person: `data/counts.json`, written on a full online run, pass or fail. The checklist now runs prerender, the gate, then prerender again, so the printed numbers are that run's. The file is not deployed.
- **D6. Each part links where it is shown or explained:** the compounds to `/compounds/`, the citations to How we check's gate section, the corrections to the log.
- **D7. "citations checked" counts citations, not sources.** 5,804 is every PubMed, PMC and DOI occurrence on 180 pages; the same paper cited on ten pages counts ten times. How we check says so in its own words ("Every PubMed ID … on every page").

**The author box and About**

- **D8. One box, the same everywhere,** generated from one string, on compound pages only. Stack and pair pages don't carry it: they are lookups over the compound pages, which carry it.
- **D9. It sits outside the reading surface,** on black, after the reading column closes, so it reads as the site speaking rather than part of the page's own text.
- **D10. About opens with the box's words,** from the same string, so the two can never drift apart; its old dek ("Peptides for regular people. Independent, cited, nothing to sell.") is gone with the phrase.
- **D11. About's rule 3 keeps its number but takes G's words,** "No vendor pays for a word of it", and loses the explanatory line under it ("No paid tier, no ebooks…"), which only restated "nothing to sell". The README's three rules match.

**How we check**

- **D12. Every claim on it is one the repo can show today,** and the live numbers are generated blocks, not prose. Where a number comes from a past session's record (the dosing pass's 3,429 edits, 12f's 482 findings), it is attributed to what was done, in the past tense.
- **D13. The honest paragraph names machines and a person,** in G's terms, and does not name any model, tool or vendor.
- **D14. It describes, never instructs.** The only second-person sentence is the reader's way to send a correction.
- **D15. No vendor names,** the analytics tool included: the disclaimer calls it "a cookieless analytics tool", as the other pages do.

**The cards**

- **D16. MONTHLY SHOT covers every 4 weeks or rarer** (MariTide's 4 or 8 weeks, triptorelin's 4 weeks to 6 months). **SHOT covers as needed and no one schedule** (PT-141 before sex; ACE-031 every 2 or 4 weeks). ACE-031 goes to SHOT rather than a stretched WEEKLY SHOT or MONTHLY SHOT, because neither is true of both of its schedules.
- **D17. The 16 compounds and the Mito Stack with no word keep none.** A wrong word is worse than none, and G's brief named only the four.
- **D18. A link's group is the compound's.** Where a link named one group and pointed at another, the compound's own group wins; where the compound is in the group the link named, that group stays, even when it is a secondary one.
- **D19. The reseed.** 147 pages' dates were reseeded, as the brief says: the link's text is a name and an address, not a claim. The two prose mentions went the same way (Title Case of a group name the page already named).
- **D20. Bioregulators keeps its chip, its page and its count,** and loses only its section of cards. A class that filters is still one click from every member, and no compound's card appears twice.
- **D21. "Also relevant" never lists a class.** The class's page shows the cards themselves, so a link would be a weaker duplicate.
- **D22. The N-acetyl meters are 0, not 1.** "Animal only" would still be the parent's record: no animal study of the capped molecules is published either. The scope line's second half is the card's meter, so it reads "No data for this form".
- **D23. Only N-Acetyl-Epithalon's hero stamp changed.** The other two read "Clinical Use (Russia)", which is their parents' own verdict; the new line says whose it is. N-Acetyl-Epithalon's "Moderate Evidence" was a verdict no page gave, so it takes Epithalon's.
- **D24. A card's date is its own page's,** and it is left out of the hash of every page that shows the card, so one compound's edit can't move another page's "Checked". Hub pages are the exception, as they were: their dates move with their cards.
- **D25. Bromantane: cut, not kept with a flag.** Truth 3c left the year flagged because it was not on that session's list; this brief put it on the list, and no source read gives it. The approval itself is cited twice.
- **D26. A new reference rather than a bare assertion.** The 2012 review is cited for "certified in Russia as a drug" because the page now rests the approval on the register plus one peer-reviewed source.

**Dates, process and the deploy**

- **D27. Which pages' dates moved.** Only N-Acetyl-Epithalon's (its hero stamp is page content) and the pages already dated today (About, the homepage, `/compounds/`, the goal pages, bromantane, N-Acetyl Selank). The other two N-acetyl pages' meters and scope lines are data and generated blocks, so their dates stayed, as the site's rule says — the corrections entries record the change either way.
- **D28. One agent, as the brief says.** No independent verifier read this session's hand-written text, against my standing note. What I did instead: read each corrections entry against the page's old and new text, and each How we check claim against the repo or the report it came from.
- **D29. The deploy went out twice,** 13 seconds apart, from the same files. The first `vercel --prod` ran with its output piped to `tail`, which hid the deployment ID, and I ran it again to read it. The second is live; the first is superseded. The same slip as the cards session, now in my notes as: run it once, alone, then read the ID with `vercel inspect`.
- **D30. The report is dated 2026-10-12,** as the brief asks. Pages, data and deploy say 2026-10-11, the day the work ran.
