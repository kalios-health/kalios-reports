### Part 1 — the card gate (`58aaf54e`)

`scripts/card_gate.py` reads every card the site draws — `/compounds/`, the goal pages, the homepage, `/stacks/`, the stack and pair pages' receipts, and `/stack.html`'s receipt data, which its script draws — 553 drawings of 156 cards, and holds each to its page and the data:

| # | Check | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | placement | once on `/compounds/` and once across the use-group goal pages, in its primary group; a stack on `/compounds/` only; the Bioregulators hub shows exactly its members |
| 2 | hook-group | the hook doesn't negate its group's use word in the same clause (`hook_group.negations`) |
| 3 | verdict | hero verdict and meter one level through one table (`card_data.BADGE_LEVEL`), unless a scope line matches the data, names the meter and, where it says "for this use", agrees with it |
| 4 | stamp | FDA APPROVED only with an application on record, COMPOUNDED RX only with a basis and its FDA source, GRAY MARKET otherwise; a stack its worst member's; every drawn stamp, hero legal line and status line in the data's words |
| 5 | for-another | "for another use" exactly when an FDA APPROVED approval isn't for the card's tag |
| 6 | route | the word is `data/routes.json`'s; the dosing section says how it's taken (and how often, for a daily, weekly or monthly shot); where it names no route, the page's own text must |
| 7 | date | "Checked" is the page's date |
| 8 | red-flag | a flag row exactly when the data has one, in its words, those words found on the page; a box titled for a label's boxed warning means a flag |
| 9 | hook | the hook is the page's own (`data/compound-desc.json`) and gives no instruction |
| 10 | name-link | the name is the page's H1; the link opens a live page |

A finding passes only if `data/card-gate-allow.json` lists it with a reason, as the citation gate's allowlist works. `--selftest` plants one failure of each kind on a copy of the site — a second BPC-157 card in Skin & Hair, a GLP-1 hook denying weight loss, TB-500's verdict raised to Phase III, KPV's card stamped FDA APPROVED, SS-31 without "for another use", BPC-157 as a pill, a wrong Checked date, MK-677 without its flag, an imperative hook, a dead link — and catches all ten. ### Part 3 — the status diff (`58f89d77`)

- `scripts/status_diff.py` snapshots every card's stamp (by meaning), legal line, hero verdict, meter and red flag, every page with its name, and the events on record — from the working tree, or from any commit through git. Each prerender compares the site with `data/status-snapshot.json` and keeps the differences in `data/changes.json`: date, compound or page, what changed, from, to, the event behind it where one is recorded, and whether it's a trigger. A rebuild keeps an entry's date; a change undone before the deploy drops out.
- From that file: the homepage's "what changed" strip shows the newest three; a compound or stack page whose stamp or legal line changed in the last 90 days says "status changed {date} →" in its strip; `/changes.html` lists everything newest first (combination pages retired on one day are one line); `/feed.xml` is RSS 2.0 of the same, the newest 100, served as `application/rss+xml`.
- The snapshot moves only after a successful deploy: `python3 scripts/status_diff.py snapshot --deploy dpl_…`, the checklist's last step.

## DECISIONS

Conservative throughout; no questions asked.

**Part 0**
- **D1. The mark at Karl's size, by the mark's own rule.** 72 px is under 96, so the page draws `mark-solid.svg` on a phone and `mark.svg` at 96 px on desktop. On the share card the mark took his width (140) on his baseline; the card's own 96 px mark, top right, stays, so the card now carries the K twice (open question 2).
- **D2. Karl's files are deleted, not just unreferenced,** so nothing of him is deployed. The design artboards that draw him are in `kalios-design/`, which never deploys, and are history.
- **D3. Scope lines from each page's words.** Glutathione's names the precursor and its use as the page does (NAC, Tylenol overdose); noopept and Semax take the brief's phrase, a Russian registration. No corrections entries: a scope line says what a stamp rates; nothing that stood was false.
- **D4. Level M without forcing a version.** The encoder still takes the smallest version that holds the URL: version 4 for 106 compound URLs, version 5 for 52. Forcing version 5 for all would shrink the short slugs' modules for no gain; every QR keeps the same 0.56 in box.
- **D5. The 1 px overlap is site-wide,** on the compound pages too: the hairline is a property of the edge, not of the article pages.

**Part 1**
- **D6. An allowlist with reasons,** as the citation gate has, so the gate can fail on any finding and still let through what a person has checked; an entry that matches nothing is reported.
- **D7. "The page's hook"** is the hook recorded for the page in `data/compound-desc.json`: no compound page prints its card's hook, so the gate holds every drawn card to that record.
- **D8. "The page has a flag"** is read two ways: the data's flag, its words found on the page; and a box titled for a label's boxed warning, FDA's strongest. Ordinary label warnings (teduglutide's) and a boxed warning since removed (abaloparatide's) don't count.
- **D9. Where a route word and a dosing section disagree, the word stands and is asked about.** The cards session's rule (G) is the form a compound is sold in; four cards follow it while their dosing sections give injected or community routes. Changing them would overrule G's rule on my own.
- **D10. An instruction** is an imperative at a sentence's start or a directive modal. "Lifters inject it" describes.
- **D11. "Strong Preclinical" → level 1** is a table fix, not a judgment about LL-37: preclinical is animal and lab data by the meter's own definition.
- **D12. Thymalin's scope line** is the gate's own remedy (a scope line where the page puts its human data on another use), in the brief's pattern; its wording is mine (open question 5).
- **D13. Five red flags changed or added, no corrections entries:** none of the old words was false; each change is on `/changes.html` with its old words.
- **D14. The Bioregulators hub** is the documented exception to "once across the goal pages" (G, trust).

**Part 2**
- **D15. A regulatory event is a regulator's action:** FDA and its committee, dockets and Federal Register notices, and foreign medicine regulators. Left out, as the file's note says: search and reading dates, labelers' NDC listings, companies' filings and lawsuits, the HHS secretary's February 2026 podcast remarks (the pages call them remarks, not a rule), WADA's lists, anything before 2025.
- **D16. The tracker shows the US events only;** it is the FDA tracker. The file keeps the six foreign ones.
- **D17. Sources as the site gives them.** Where a page names a document without an address (the warning letters, Forzinity's label, press releases), the source link is that page's reference list ("Source, as cited"); a DailyMed set-ID page where the page cites a label by its set ID (both checked to resolve). No Federal Register or regulations.gov address was formed: both refuse automated checks.
- **D18. Before and after in plain words,** because most events predate the stance lines; the `card` field records the line an event put on the cards.
- **D19. The February 2027 meeting is a scheduled event,** shown first on the timeline and dated by its deadline; the tracker's date moves with its content, so it is 11 Oct 2026 now.

**Part 3**
- **D20. Stamps compared by meaning:** COMPOUNDING PHARMACY and COMPOUNDED RX are one stamp, so the cards session's rename is no change.
- **D21. An entry's date** is the day a build first found it; the backfill's, the day its deploy went live. The pages show that date; the strip shows the event's where one is recorded — the brief's own example, "status changed 1 Oct 2026" — and the 90 days count from it.
- **D22. Public words only.** Retired pages are described by kind ("merged into another page…"), and the prune's combination pages by its rule, never by `data/retired-pages.json`'s internal notes, which `data/changes.json` doesn't carry.
- **D23. The feed is dated by its entries,** so a build that finds nothing writes the same file; the newest 100.
- **D24. The snapshot, the changes and the events files aren't deployed** (`.vercelignore`): no page fetches them, and `/changes.html`, `/feed.xml` and the tracker are their public forms.

**Part 4**
- **D25. "Pre-prune" read as the October 4 deploy.** A snapshot from after October 5 can't show the October 5 change the brief names.
- **D26. One backfill over the deploys in between,** so each entry carries the date it went live. That also records the brief's "group rename" as it happened (GLP-1 & Weight-Loss Shots, then GLP-1 & Amylin Shots) rather than collapsing it.
- **D27. The first draft names the pairs and the rename** though they aren't triggers, because the brief asked for them in it; the rest is a count. Later drafts do the same: triggers first, then the notable pages, then a count.
- **D28. One draft per deploy cycle,** named for the day it was written and its first status change.
- **D29. The Alerts page keeps G's three bullets** and adds one for a legal line that names an FDA action, a trigger the old list didn't cover. The in-page alert form on research-only compound pages stays.
- **D30. One agent, as the brief says.** No independent verifier read this session's hand-written text, against my standing note. What I did instead: held each event sentence to the page it came from, and each flag and scope line to its page's words, through the gate.
- **D31. The report is named 2026-10-13,** as the brief asks; pages, data and deploy say 2026-10-11, the day the work ran.
