## DECISIONS

### Part 1: verb pass

1. **Scope.**
   - Sections whose heading names Dosing, Reconstitution, Storage, Cycle, Practical User Notes, Cost & Access or Bloodwork.
   - Also "Sample Weekly Schedule" (dosing), "Objective Readouts to Track" (bloodwork) and the Cost & Access quick fact.
   - "Side Effects & Monitoring" is out of scope.
2. **Some imperatives kept.**
   - Clinician referrals ("consult…", "work with…") stay, because CLAUDE.md requires "consult a licensed healthcare provider".
   - Editorial "See / Read" cues stay.
   - Modal *should / must* sentences stay too, since they aren't imperatives (open question 8).
3. **Rewrite patterns.**
   - "Practitioner reports describe…"
   - "Oral use is typically described…"
   - "Reconstitution guides describe…"
   - "Labels commonly advise…"
   - "Formulation guides…"
   - "commonly repeated at … in practitioner protocols"
   - Imperative bold labels became noun phrases. Red-flag lists end with "These are stop-and-evaluate signals."
4. **"Purchase only…" sentences.**
   - The two on bpc-157 and cjc-1295 became the approved practical-floor COA sentence. They keep "above 98%" and "HPLC purity + MS identity" but drop the named labs.
   - 6 other vendor-directed lines ("Trust only…", "Insist on COA"…) were converted the same way. Practical-floor sentences went from 19 to 27.
5. **Calculator and Stack Builder links.**
   - 118 calculator links now read "→ Peptide Calculator — vial-to-syringe math", with no trailing period. Specific descriptors were kept, e.g. "parenteral conversions".
   - 2 in-scope Stack Builder links now read "→ Stack Builder — compound compatibility".
6. **/stacks/ sentence.** It was changed in the visible text and in its FAQ JSON-LD twin, which the audit whitelists by exact string.
7. **FDA-approved pages (18).** Storage and freeze lines use passive, attribution-free wording instead of "Labels commonly advise". A label attribution could misstate the real label. EGRIFTA SV's label says room temperature, yet the page says refrigerate (open question 3).

### Part 2: Category 2 truth audit

8. **Date correction.** I used "Removed from Category 2 on April 22, 2026 (FDA notice April 15, 2026)", not the brief's "April 15".
   - FDA's list "Updated April 15, 2026" gave seven days' notice.
   - The "Updated April 22, 2026" version says "has been removed". Both versions are archived on web.archive.org.
   - The PCAC wording is verbatim from the brief: "PCAC recommended it on July 23–24, 2026. Rulemaking pending — not legal to compound today."
   - For DSIP: "PCAC voted against it on July 23–24, 2026. Not legal to compound today."
9. **More truth classes than the brief's three, because FDA's May 14 list needs them.** The sentences are generated by `scripts/category_truth.py` from regulatory-status.json; the full table is `gsc-export/category-truth.csv`.
   - **503A Category 2:** Kisspeptin-10 and ibutamoren (MK-677), both since 2023-09-29.
   - **503A Category 3:** ARA-290, GHRP-2, GHRP-6, MGF and thymulin. GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 are also on 503B Category 2.
   - **Formerly Category 2, nominations withdrawn:** AOD-9604, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, selank and thymosin α1. Ipamorelin is still on 503B Category 2.
   - **PCAC review due before the end of February 2027:** dihexa, LL-37, Melanotan II, PEG-MGF and injectable GHK-Cu.
   - **Category 1:** non-injectable GHK-Cu. It was removed April 22 and re-added May 14 after a nominator's May 5 clarification.
   - **Compoundable as a component of an approved drug:** sermorelin (GEREF) and gonadorelin (Lutrepulse / Factrel).
10. **Sentence forms.**
    - Running prose names the compound as the subject.
    - Quick facts, table cells and pair cards use short field forms of 60 characters or less, which is the pair card's display limit.
11. **Edit mechanics.**

    - Where the rest of a sentence was accurate, only the clause changed.
